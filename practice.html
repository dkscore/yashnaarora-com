Prep for Revolut

Everything here maps to Revolut's actual hiring stages. Work through the aptitude test until you are fast, rehearse the behavioural answers against their values, and practise the design rounds. Start with whichever stage is next for you.

Aptitude practice

Revolut uses the CCAT: 50 questions in 15 minutes, roughly 18 seconds each, no calculator. The skill is speed and knowing when to skip. Run full timed simulations, and drill weak categories untimed with explanations.

Practice by category

Pick an area, see the answer and explanation after each question. Build accuracy first, then add speed.

How to score well
  • You are not expected to finish all 50. Answer accurately and keep moving; do not sink 90 seconds into one question.
  • There is no penalty for wrong answers, so never leave a question blank at the very end. Guess the last few.
  • Numerical questions rarely need exact maths. Estimate and eliminate options.
  • Do the quick verbal and series questions first, flag the heavy logic for a second pass.

Design rounds

The product-designer rounds test product thinking, analytical rigour and how you handle ambiguity. Rehearse each out loud until the structure is automatic.

Round 1 · Product sense

"Design or improve a feature for Revolut"

Do not jump to a screen. Walk them through a structure:

  • Clarify the goal and who it is for. Which user, which problem, what does success look like.
  • Users and needs. Name a segment and their real pain, ideally from how Revolut actually works.
  • Prioritise the one problem worth solving now, and say why the others wait.
  • Solution. Two or three ideas, pick one, sketch the flow, name the key screens or moments.
  • Trade-offs and metrics. What you are trading, and the metric that tells you it worked.
Practise on: help users save more without thinking about it; simplify the card-freeze flow; onboard a first-time international transfer; surface a subscription upgrade at the right moment. Give yourself 5 minutes each, out loud.
Round 2 · Analytical depth

KPIs, data and experiments

  • For any feature, name the one primary metric and a guardrail metric (the thing you must not harm).
  • Be ready to design an A/B test: hypothesis, what you change, what you measure, how long, what result would make you ship.
  • Tie design decisions to numbers. Your Mining Journal work is perfect here: touchpoints halved, drop-off as the metric.
Round 3 · Problem-solving case

The case where you get no data unless you ask

  • They are testing whether you ask before assuming. Open by asking clarifying questions and stating your assumptions out loud.
  • Structure first: break the problem into parts, then go deep on the part that matters most.
  • Think in ranges and estimates. Show your reasoning, not a memorised answer.
Round 4 · Bar Raiser

One achievement, probed two or three layers deep

  • Pick two projects you can go very deep on. HMRC notification strategy and the Mining Journal redesign are your strongest.
  • For each, know: the exact problem, your specific role versus the team, why you made each decision, what the outcome was, and what you would do differently.
  • They will keep asking "why" and "what was your part". Vague or borrowed credit is what fails this round, so be precise about what was yours.
