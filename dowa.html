The project

DOWA is a functional AI product prototype I designed and built independently. It organises research, synthesises evidence, surfaces patterns, challenges assumptions and suggests next-step experiments, while the design team keeps every final product and UX decision.

The problem

Design time disappears into operations

Across research-heavy projects, a large share of a design team's week goes into work that is necessary but repetitive. The thinking that actually moves a product forward gets squeezed by the operations around it.

Reviewing and organising research across interviews, surveys and boards.

Synthesising large amounts of information into something a team can act on.

Maintaining evidence and context so decisions stay traceable weeks later.

Identifying patterns and contradictions scattered across sources.

Preparing material for design reviews and team discussions.

Deciding what to investigate next when the evidence is incomplete.

The solution

An AI teammate inside the team's workflow

DOWA lives where the team already works: a shared workspace with the project brief, research resources, channels and a design lifecycle. You attach evidence, DOWA does the processing, and the output arrives as a reviewable analysis the team can question, correct and build on. AI handles the repetitive analysis and execution. The designers keep the judgement.

Evidence in, analysis out. DOWA works only from the files you attach, cites them in its findings, and says plainly when the selected evidence cannot support an answer.

✦Research synthesis on demand

Attach interviews, surveys or journey boards and DOWA groups behaviours, needs and frustrations, preserving the supporting evidence for every claim.

✦Design Memory

Findings, decisions and open questions persist across the project. Later analyses cite earlier ones, so context stops living in people's heads.

✦Challenges assumptions

Disagree with DOWA and it re-tests its own claim against the evidence, names what would falsify it, and concedes the points it cannot support.

✦Suggests the next experiment

Every analysis ends with a prepared next step: what to investigate, which discussion to have, or which evidence gap to close first.

✦The team decides

DOWA prepares; it never decides. Asked to pick the solution, it declines and lays out the trade-offs instead. Final product and UX calls stay with the designers.

✦Honest about its limits

Unreadable files are named, thin evidence is flagged, and "insufficient evidence" is a legitimate answer rather than a failure.

Design & AI development process

From concept to working product, with AI

I acted as the product designer and decision-maker, coordinating two AI tools with deliberately different jobs. The loop below ran continuously: every feature went from direction, through critique, to working code, and back through my review.

1

Product concept and UX direction

I defined what DOWA is, the principle that it supports designers rather than replacing them, and the key workflows: evidence in, reviewable analysis out, memory across the project.

2

GPT‑5.6 Sol · reasoning and design critique

Used for exploring product directions, evaluating design alternatives, critiquing UX and interaction decisions, and challenging my assumptions before anything was built.

3

My evaluation and design decisions

I weighed the critiques against the product intent, rejected what did not serve it, and committed the direction. Every decision that shipped is one I can defend.

4

Claude Code · implementation and testing

Used for building the functional experience, executing development tasks and testing: the workspace, the analysis engine, evidence provenance, memory and the conversational layer.

5

Iteration

I tested the working product as a designer would use it, found what broke or confused, and sent the loop around again. The prototype shipped only when the experience held up end to end.

Key learnings

What building with AI taught me

AI collapses the distance from idea to working product

Concepts that would once have stayed as static mockups became a testable, functional experience in days. The faster the prototype, the earlier the real design problems show up.

Different tools earn different jobs

A reasoning model that critiques and challenges is not the same tool as one that executes and builds. Splitting the roles kept the thinking sharp and the implementation fast.

AI output needs a critical reviewer

Both tools produced convincing work that was sometimes wrong. Reviewing everything against the product intent, and rejecting what did not hold, was the job that mattered most.

Decisions must stay with designers

The clearest lesson is the product's own principle: AI is at its best preparing the ground. Judgement about users, trade-offs and direction has to remain a human responsibility.
